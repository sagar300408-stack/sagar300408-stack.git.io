80 Businesses. 80 Problems. Why We’re Starting With Questions, Not Technology

Originyx 2047 | 80 Businesses → 80 Problems

India’s journey toward becoming a developed nation by 2047 will not be built by technology companies alone.

It will be built by millions of businesses becoming more productive, more capable, and better equipped to compete.

That raises a more practical question:

What prevents businesses from operating better today?

At Originyx, we believe the answer cannot be found by sitting behind a screen and designing another product.

It has to be found by going into businesses, observing how they actually work, understanding where they struggle, and identifying what can genuinely be improved.

That is why we are starting 80 Businesses → 80 Problems.

## We are starting with the problem

Technology has created an unusual temptation for builders.

We can build faster than ever.

AI can generate software. Automation can connect systems. APIs can eliminate repetitive tasks. New tools appear every week.

But there is a danger hidden inside all this capability:

We can become very good at building solutions to problems that nobody actually needs solved.

A business does not care whether a solution uses AI, automation, Python, or the latest technology stack.

The business cares about whether something gets better.

Does a process become faster?

Does an employee spend less time on repetitive work?

Are fewer opportunities lost?

Do errors decrease?

Does the owner finally have visibility into what is happening?

Does the business save money or serve customers better?

Those are the questions that matter.

So our operating principle is simple:

> Find the problem first. Understand it deeply. Build only when technology can create genuine value.

## What is 80 Businesses → 80 Problems?

The initiative is our first practical step toward the broader Originyx 2047 vision.

We intend to engage with Indian businesses across different industries and understand how their operations actually work.

We are looking for problems such as:

* Repetitive manual work

* Inefficient workflows

* Lost opportunities

* Delayed processes

* Poor information flow

* Operational bottlenecks

* Lack of visibility

* Avoidable errors

* Inefficient customer or internal processes

But identifying a problem is only the beginning.

We want to understand why the problem exists.

What happens today?

Who is involved?

How frequently does it occur?

What does it cost in time or effort?

What happens when something goes wrong?

Is the problem caused by technology, process, people, information, management, or something else?

Only after understanding those questions can we determine what should happen next.

## Observe → Understand → Diagnose → Determine → Improve → Measure

This is the process we want to follow.

Observe.

See how work actually happens instead of relying entirely on assumptions.

Understand.

Learn the context behind the workflow and the people operating it.

Diagnose.

Identify the underlying operational problem rather than simply describing the symptom.

Determine.

Decide whether the problem needs technology, a process change, better information, or something else.

Improve.

Implement the appropriate intervention where meaningful value can be created.

Measure.

Determine whether anything actually became better.

This last step matters.

A technology deployment is not automatically an improvement.

If a new system is installed but employees still spend the same amount of time doing the same work, the technology has not solved the problem.

## Not every problem needs AI

This initiative is deliberately different from an AI-first approach.

We are not entering a business assuming that every problem should become an AI product.

Some problems may be solved with a simple process change.

Some may require better documentation.

Some may require training.

Some may require management changes or additional resources.

Others may reveal genuine opportunities for automation, software, artificial intelligence, or operational intelligence.

All of these outcomes are valuable.

If we discover that technology cannot solve a particular problem, we have still learned something important.

If we discover that a simple operational change can solve it, that is useful knowledge.

And if we discover an opportunity where technology can create measurable value, that becomes a potential technology opportunity.

The objective is not to maximize the number of products we build.

The objective is to maximize real-world improvement.

## Why 80 businesses?

Eighty is not a claim that 80 businesses can represent India's entire business ecosystem.

They cannot.

India's businesses are too diverse for that.

The 80-business target is a deliberate first milestone: large enough to expose us to different operational realities, but focused enough to make the commitment measurable.

Our intended progression is:

80 Businesses

↓

80 Real Problems

↓

80 Diagnoses

↓

Selected Technology Opportunities

↓

Measured Improvements

And what we learn will determine what comes next.

## A mission for young builders

There is another reason this matters to us.

India's young developers have access to extraordinary technology.

But knowing how to build software is only one part of becoming a useful technology builder.

We also need to learn how to observe, question, understand, build, and measure.

A developer who can understand a real business problem and create measurable value is doing more than writing software.

They are contributing to productive capacity.

We want Originyx to be built around that mindset.

## Toward Originyx 2047

We do not know what India's business landscape will look like in 2047.

We do not want to pretend that we can predict it.

Instead, we want to participate in building it.

Our long-term ambition is straightforward:

> Make Every Business Better.

If Indian businesses become more productive, if smaller businesses gain access to sophisticated technology, if repetitive operational work is reduced, if businesses make better decisions with better information, and if technology adoption becomes increasingly driven by measurable value rather than hype, then we believe we are contributing in the direction we want.

Originyx cannot build Viksit Bharat by itself.

That is not the claim.

Our contribution is much more practical:

Understand businesses. Find the problems holding them back. Solve what we can. Measure what changes. Learn. Repeat.

And perhaps the journey toward 2047 should begin exactly there.

Not with a prediction about the future.

Not with another technology demonstration.

But with a question asked inside a real business:

“What is preventing this business from operating better?”
